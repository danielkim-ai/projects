# Theory: Trustworthy Offline RL via Differential Privacy and Machine Unlearning

This note formalizes the statistical assumptions behind the prototype pipeline:

```text
offline trajectories -> trajectory DP-SGD -> deletion request -> unlearning audit
```

The intended deployment setting is sensitive sequential data, such as healthcare care paths, finance interaction logs, or long-horizon human feedback traces. In such data, a single person's contribution is rarely one independent row. It is often a correlated trajectory.

## 1. Offline RL Setup and Privacy Tension

Let an offline dataset be a finite population of trajectories

$$
\mathcal{D} = \{\tau_i\}_{i=1}^{n},
\qquad
\tau_i = (s_{i,t}, a_{i,t}, r_{i,t}, s'_{i,t}, d_{i,t})_{t=0}^{T_i-1}.
$$

The data are generated by an unknown behavior policy $\mu$:

$$
\tau \sim p_\mu(\tau)
= \rho_0(s_0)\prod_{t=0}^{T-1}
\mu(a_t \mid s_t)P(s_{t+1}\mid s_t,a_t).
$$

The learner evaluates or optimizes a target policy $\pi$ under a different induced distribution:

$$
J(\pi) = \mathbb{E}_{\tau \sim p_\pi}
\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\gamma^t r(s_t,a_t)\right].
$$

Offline RL is difficult because the support of $p_\pi$ may not be contained in the support of $p_\mu$. The covariate-shift condition is

$$
d^\pi(s,a) \neq d^\mu(s,a),
$$

where $d^\pi$ is the state-action occupancy measure. If $Q_\theta(s,a)$ is queried outside the empirical support, bootstrapped Bellman targets may overestimate OOD actions.

### Why Transition-Level Adjacency Is Insufficient

Transition-level adjacency treats two datasets as adjacent if they differ by one transition:

$$
\mathcal{D} \sim_{\text{trans}} \mathcal{D}'
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
|\mathcal{D}\triangle \mathcal{D}'| = 1.
$$

This definition assumes transition records are the protected unit. In sequential data this is generally false. A user's record is the trajectory $\tau_i$, whose transitions are temporally correlated:

$$
\operatorname{Cov}(g_{i,t}, g_{i,t+k}) \neq 0,
\qquad
g_{i,t} = \nabla_\theta \ell(\theta; s_{i,t},a_{i,t},r_{i,t},s'_{i,t}).
$$

The trajectory gradient is

$$
G_i = \sum_{t=0}^{T_i-1} g_{i,t}.
$$

Its second moment is

$$
\mathbb{E}\|G_i\|_2^2
=
\sum_t \mathbb{E}\|g_{i,t}\|_2^2
+ 2\sum_{t<u}\mathbb{E}\langle g_{i,t}, g_{i,u}\rangle.
$$

Transition-level clipping ignores the covariance sum. If correlations are positive, the true trajectory sensitivity can grow faster than the independent-transition approximation:

$$
\Delta_{\text{traj}}
= \max_{\tau,\tau'}\|\nabla \ell(\tau)-\nabla \ell(\tau')\|_2
\not\approx
\max_t \|g_t-g'_t\|_2.
$$

Thus transition-level DP may overstate privacy protection because it calibrates noise to a smaller adjacency unit than the legal and ethical deletion unit. Trajectory-level adjacency instead defines

$$
\mathcal{D} \sim_{\text{traj}} \mathcal{D}'
\quad \Longleftrightarrow \quad
\mathcal{D}' = \mathcal{D}\setminus \{\tau_k\}
\quad\text{or}\quad
\mathcal{D}' = \mathcal{D}\cup \{\tau_k\}.
$$

This is the adjacency model used by the optimizer in this project.

## 2. DP Mechanism and Privacy-Utility Frontier

For a mini-batch of trajectories $B$, define the per-trajectory loss

$$
L_i(\theta) = \sum_{t=0}^{T_i-1}\ell(\theta; z_{i,t}),
\qquad z_{i,t}=(s_{i,t},a_{i,t},r_{i,t},s'_{i,t},d_{i,t}).
$$

The trajectory gradient is

$$
G_i = \nabla_\theta L_i(\theta).
$$

The DP-SGD release clips each trajectory gradient:

$$
\bar{G}_i
=
G_i \cdot \min\left(1,\frac{C}{\|G_i\|_2}\right),
$$

then aggregates and perturbs:

$$
\tilde{G}
=
\frac{1}{|B|}
\left(
\sum_{i\in B}\bar{G}_i
+ \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2 C^2 I)
\right).
$$

The update is

$$
\theta_{k+1}=\theta_k-\eta\tilde{G}.
$$

### RDP Composition

For the Gaussian mechanism with noise multiplier $\sigma$, the order-$\alpha$ RDP cost is bounded by

$$
\varepsilon_\alpha^{\text{Gauss}}
=
\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^2}.
$$

With subsampling rate $q=|B|/n$, the implementation uses a lightweight approximation

$$
\varepsilon_\alpha^{\text{sub}}
\approx
q^2\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^2}.
$$

After $K$ optimization steps, RDP composes additively:

$$
\varepsilon_\alpha^{(K)}
=
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varepsilon_{\alpha,k}
=
Kq^2\frac{\alpha}{2\sigma^2}.
$$

Conversion to $(\varepsilon,\delta)$-DP is

$$
\varepsilon(\delta)
=
\min_{\alpha>1}
\left[
\varepsilon_\alpha^{(K)}
+ \frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\alpha-1}
\right].
$$

This produces the privacy budget reported by `PrivacyAuditor`.

### CQL Under Gaussian Noise

CQL controls OOD overestimation by penalizing the gap between values assigned to policy or sampled actions and values assigned to dataset actions. A simplified continuous-action penalty is

$$
\mathcal{R}_{\text{CQL}}(\theta)
=
\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{D}}
\left[
\log \int_{\mathcal{A}}\exp(Q_\theta(s,a))da
- Q_\theta(s,a_{\mathcal{D}})
\right].
$$

DP noise perturbs the learned critic. Write

$$
\hat{Q}(s,a)=Q_\theta(s,a)+\xi(s,a),
\qquad
\xi\sim \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_Q^2).
$$

For $m$ sampled OOD actions and sub-Gaussian noise, a union bound gives

$$
\Pr\left[
\max_{j\le m}\xi_j
>
\sigma_Q\sqrt{2\log(m/\beta)}
\right]\le \beta.
$$

Thus an OOD-safe critic should include a concentration penalty:

$$
\mathcal{R}_{\text{privacy}}(\theta)
=
(1+\sigma)
\mathbb{E}
\left[
\max(0, \max_j \hat{Q}(s,a_j^{\text{ood}})-\hat{Q}(s,a_{\mathcal{D}}))^2
\right].
$$

If the Bellman operator is $\gamma$-contractive in the sup norm,

$$
\|\mathcal{T}Q_1-\mathcal{T}Q_2\|_\infty
\le
\gamma\|Q_1-Q_2\|_\infty,
$$

then pessimistic regularization keeps noisy OOD maximization from dominating the target. The penalty acts as a high-probability concentration correction that shrinks optimistic tails while preserving the contraction argument on the supported action region.

## 3. Mathematical Formalization of Three Unlearning Strategies

### Strategy 1: Influence Function Unlearning

Let empirical risk be

$$
R(\theta)
=
\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^n L_i(\theta),
\qquad
\hat{\theta}=\arg\min_\theta R(\theta).
$$

Removing $\tau_k$ induces

$$
R_{-k}(\theta)
=
\frac{1}{n-1}\sum_{i\ne k} L_i(\theta).
$$

The first-order influence approximation is

$$
\hat{\theta}_{-k}-\hat{\theta}
\approx
\frac{1}{n}
H_{\hat{\theta}}^{-1}\nabla_\theta L_k(\hat{\theta}),
$$

where

$$
H_{\hat{\theta}}
=
\nabla_\theta^2 R(\hat{\theta}).
$$

Deep RL objectives are non-convex and Hessians can be indefinite. The damped system is

$$
(H_{\hat{\theta}}+\lambda I)v
=
\nabla_\theta L_k(\hat{\theta}),
\qquad \lambda>0.
$$

LiSSA approximates $v=(H+\lambda I)^{-1}g$ recursively. With scale $s$ and initial $v_0=g$:

$$
v_{t+1}
=
g
+ \left(I-\frac{H+\lambda I}{s}\right)v_t.
$$

The update used for removal is

$$
\theta'
=
\theta+\eta_u v,
$$

optionally with unlearning noise

$$
\theta'
=
\theta+\eta_u(v+\zeta),
\qquad
\zeta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_u^2 I).
$$

The damping term controls curvature instability by lower-bounding eigenvalues of the modified Hessian.

### Strategy 2: Certified DP Without Additional Action

Differential Privacy already bounds the distinguishability between models trained on adjacent trajectory datasets:

$$
\Pr[\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D})\in S]
\le
e^\varepsilon
\Pr[\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}\setminus\tau_k)\in S]
+\delta.
$$

For pure DP, one can relate this to total variation:

$$
d_{\mathcal{TV}}(P,Q)
=
\sup_S |P(S)-Q(S)|
\le
e^\varepsilon-1.
$$

For approximate DP, a common relaxed certificate is

$$
d_{\mathcal{TV}}
\left(
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}),
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{D}\setminus\tau_k)
\right)
\le
e^\varepsilon-1+\delta.
$$

If the deployed model was produced by a sufficiently strong trajectory-level DP mechanism, deletion may be certified without modifying parameters. This is not a universal legal answer; it is a mathematical indistinguishability argument that must be interpreted alongside retention policy, audit logs, and applicable regulation.

Ethically, no-action certification is acceptable only when the privacy budget is low enough for the affected domain and the data controller can explain what remains inferable. In healthcare or finance, this must be paired with provenance and access control rather than treated as a replacement for governance.

### Strategy 3: SISA Sharding

SISA partitions the trajectory dataset into disjoint shards:

$$
\mathcal{D}
=
\bigsqcup_{j=1}^{K}\mathcal{D}_j,
\qquad
\mathcal{D}_j\cap\mathcal{D}_{j'}=\emptyset
\quad (j\ne j').
$$

Episode-level assignment is

$$
s(\tau_i)= i \bmod K,
\qquad
\tau_i\in\mathcal{D}_{s(\tau_i)}.
$$

Each shard trains a sub-policy $\pi_j$. A deletion request for $\tau_k$ affects only shard $s(\tau_k)$:

$$
\mathcal{D}_{s(\tau_k)}'
=
\mathcal{D}_{s(\tau_k)}\setminus\{\tau_k\}.
$$

The serving policy may be an ensemble

$$
\pi_{\text{ens}}(a\mid s)
=
\frac{1}{K}\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j(a\mid s),
$$

or a distilled student

$$
\min_\phi
\mathbb{E}_{s\sim\mathcal{B}}
\left[
D\left(
\pi_\phi(\cdot\mid s),
\pi_{\text{ens}}(\cdot\mid s)
\right)
\right].
$$

If each shard mechanism is $(\alpha,\varepsilon_{\alpha,j})$-RDP and the ensemble releases all sub-policies, composition is

$$
\varepsilon_\alpha^{\text{ensemble}}
=
\sum_{j=1}^{K}\varepsilon_{\alpha,j}.
$$

If only one affected shard is retrained after deletion, the incremental privacy and compute cost is localized to that shard. Distillation is a post-processing operation if it consumes only released private sub-policies, so it does not increase the DP budget:

$$
\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{\text{distill}}
=
\varepsilon_{\alpha}^{\text{ensemble}}.
$$

This is why episode-consistent sharding is critical: splitting a trajectory across shards would corrupt both the Bellman structure and the deletion unit.
